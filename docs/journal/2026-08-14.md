## The parser was broken by its own specification

The day's real finding. REQ-1.1 has to enumerate the EARS keywords, because its
whole job is to specify how they are recognised:

> WHEN the lexer encounters the word WHEN, WHILE, IF, WHERE, THEN, SHALL …

The parser read those enumerated keywords as grammar. A criterion *about* `WHEN`
parsed as a criterion *using* `WHEN` twice, and produced a spurious empty-clause
diagnostic.

The interesting part is where the defect was visible from. Reading `lexer.py`, it
looks correct — the case rule does exactly what it claims. The bug only appears
when the tool is pointed at a real document, and the first real document happened
to be its own. This is the argument for dogfooding stated better than I could
state it in the abstract.

Fix: a backtick-delimited span lexes as one ordinary word, whatever it contains.
That honours a convention authors already follow, and it also solves
`` `SHALL NOT` `` becoming a modality plus a negation. Spec gained REQ-1.8, design
gained a section, ADR-0001 records it, five tests cover it.

## Two places I declined to guess

Both are cases where the honest answer is worse-looking than a heuristic would be.

**No `THEN`.** In `WHEN the cart is empty the system SHALL warn` there is no marker
between the end of the condition and the start of the subject. A heuristic could
guess — take the last two words before the modality — but it would be wrong
often enough to matter, and silently. The parser reads the clause body to the
modality and leaves the subject empty. The test asserts that, and says why.

I had originally written a test asserting the heuristic behaviour, then had to
choose between making the parser guess or fixing the test. Fixed the test.

**Mixed `AND` and `OR` in one clause.** `WHEN a OR b AND c` has genuinely
ambiguous precedence. Recorded as a single conjunct with no operator, rather than
inventing a tree the author did not write.

Both decisions follow from the no-model rule in steering: if a guess cannot be
justified deterministically, it does not belong in the verification path. Useful
to find that the rule earns its keep on day one, on a component that has nothing
to do with models.
